1. Executive Mission & Problem Space

1.1 The Communication and Quantification Chasm

Enterprise cybersecurity management in Fortune 500 banks and critical infrastructure is fundamentally broken by a language and measurement divide:

  • CISOs & SecOps Engineers evaluate risk using CVE counts, CVSS technical severity scores (e.g., CVSS 9.8 Critical), and 5×5 colored heatmaps (Red, Amber, Green).
  • CFOs, Boards of Directors & Risk Committees allocate capital based on real monetary currency ($ USD / ₹ INR), Expected Annual Loss (EAL), Value-at-Risk (VaR at 90%, 95%, 99%), EBITDA impact, and Return on Security Investment (ROSI).

1.2 Mathematical Failure of Qualitative 5×5 Heatmaps

Traditional enterprise risk management relies on multiplying arbitrary ordinal ratings (e.g., Likelihood "4" × Impact "5" = Score "20"):

1. Ordinal Scales Cannot Be Multiplied: Numerically, an ordinal "4" is not twice an ordinal "2"; ordinal scales have sequence but no constant interval or metric origin. Multiplying them produces mathematically meaningless numbers.

2. Range Compression & Rank Reversal: A low-frequency, existential disaster (e.g., catastrophic ransomware shutting down the SWIFT gateway causing ₹50 Crore loss) receives an identical score (e.g., 20) to a frequent nuisance event (e.g., 100 lost employee badges).

3. Arbitrary Budget Prioritization: CISOs classify all vulnerabilities as "Critical" to defend budgets, preventing CFOs from knowing where capital actually protects shareholder equity.

1.3 The LossLogic Solution

LossLogic replaces qualitative guessing with actuarial cyber risk quantification and mathematical optimization:

  • Ingests raw telemetry across 5 domains (Vulnerability/CVE/EPSS, SIEM, IAM, EDR, CSPM).
  • Propagates asset failures across a NetworkX Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) to measure cascading business revenue downtime.
  • Translates technical vulnerabilities into Open FAIR™ parameters and executes 10,000 vectorized Monte Carlo trials (<2ms per asset).
  • Allocates capital using SciPy's HiGHS Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) 0/1 Knapsack optimizer, guaranteeing mathematically optimal risk reduction under hard budget constraints.
  • Automatically crosswalks every funded mitigation across 5 regulatory frameworks: RBI CSF, SEBI CSCRF, ISO/IEC 27001, NIST CSF 2.0, and CIS Controls v8.

3. Actuarial Science & Mathematical Foundations

3.1 Open FAIR™ Parameter Translation

Located in src/quant/fair_mapper.py:

The Factor Analysis of Information Risk (Open FAIR™) framework decomposes cyber risk into probabilistic frequency and loss magnitude components:

1. Threat Contact Frequency ($CF$): Estimated frequency of external threat actor interaction:

$$CF = CF_{base} + 3.0 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{public} + 5.0 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{sensitive\_port} + 0.15 \cdot \min(50, \text{Alerts}_{24h})$$

2. Probability of Action ($PoA$): Probability an actor acts given contact, driven by EPSS and CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities:

$$PoA = \min\Big(1.0, 0.05 + 0.65 \cdot EPSS + 0.30 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{KEV} + \Delta_{maturity}\Big)$$

3. Threat Event Frequency ($TEF$):

$$TEF = CF \times PoA$$

4. Threat Capability ($TCap$):

$$TCap = \min\Big(1.0, \max\big(0.05, 1.5 \cdot EPSS + \frac{CVSS}{20.0}\big)\Big)$$

5. Resistance Strength ($RS$): Asset defensive capability degraded by findings:

$$RS = \text{clamp}\Big(RS_{base} \cdot (1 - \text{Degradation}), 0.02, 0.99\Big)$$

6. Vulnerability Probability ($Vuln$): Probability that $TCap > RS$:

$$Vuln = \max\Big(0.02, \min\big(0.98, TCap \cdot (1.0 - 0.8 \cdot RS)\big)\Big)$$

7. Loss Event Frequency ($LEF$): Annual breach frequency:

$$LEF = TEF \times Vuln$$

8. Primary Loss Magnitude: Comprises incident response ($C_{IR}$), direct downtime outage ($C_{down}$), and asset restoration ($C_{rest}$):

$$C_{IR} = 50,000 \cdot \left(\frac{CVSS}{5.0}\right)^{1.5} \cdot M_{env} \cdot ACS$$
$$C_{down} = MTTR \times \text{HourlyDowntimeRevenue}(u)$$
$$C_{rest} = 0.05 \times \text{ReplacementCost}(u)$$

9. Secondary Loss Magnitude: Statutory regulatory fines ($F_{reg}$) and litigation/churn:

$$F_{reg} = \text{BASE\_REGULATORY\_FINE} \times M_{data} \times M_{env}$$

3.2 Vectorized Compound Poisson-LogNormal Monte Carlo Engine

Located in src/quant/monte_carlo.py:

The simulation runs $N = 10,000$ stochastic annual trials per exposure:

  • Event Frequency Sampling: For each trial $j \in \{1, \dots, N\}$, the number of breach events $K_j$ is drawn from a Poisson distribution:
$$K_j \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda = LEF)$$
  • Event Loss Magnitude Sampling: Each individual breach event loss $L_{j, k}$ is sampled from a Log-Normal distribution parameterized from the three-point PERT estimate $(min, mode, max)$:
$$\mu_{pert} = \frac{min + 4 \cdot mode + max}{6}, \quad \sigma_{pert} = \frac{max - min}{6}$$
$$\mu_{log} = \ln\left(\frac{\mu_{pert}^2}{\sqrt{\sigma_{pert}^2 + \mu_{pert}^2}}\right), \quad \sigma_{log} = \sqrt{\ln\left(1 + \frac{\sigma_{pert}^2}{\mu_{pert}^2}\right)}$$
$$L_{j, k} \sim \text{Lognormal}(\mu_{log}, \sigma_{log})$$
  • High-Performance Memory-Contiguous Vectorization:

Instead of looping in Python, all $\sum_{j=1}^N K_j$ events across all trials are drawn in a single contiguous NumPy C-array and mapped to trial indices using np.repeat(np.arange(N), K_events) and np.bincount. This achieves execution latencies under 2 milliseconds per 10,000 trials.

  • Financial Risk Metrics:

- Expected Annual Loss ($EAL$): Mean annual loss:

$$EAL = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N \text{Loss}_j$$

- Value-at-Risk ($VaR_\alpha$): Empirical percentiles:

$$VaR_{90} = \text{Percentile}(\text{Losses}, 90)$$
$$VaR_{95} = \text{Percentile}(\text{Losses}, 95)$$
$$VaR_{99} = \text{Percentile}(\text{Losses}, 99)$$

- Strict Invariant Guarantee:

$$VaR_{90} < VaR_{95} < VaR_{99}$$

- Conditional VaR ($CVaR_{95}$ / Expected Shortfall): Average loss in the worst 5% tail:

$$CVaR_{95} = \mathbb{E}[\text{Loss} \mid \text{Loss} \ge VaR_{95}]$$

3.3 Multi-Tier Portfolio Aggregation & Diversification Benefit

Located in src/quant/portfolio.py:

The portfolio engine constructs a joint trial-by-trial loss matrix $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times M}$ across all $M$ assets.

  • Correlated enterprise loss vector is the row sum: $\text{Loss}_{Enterprise, j} = \sum_{m=1}^M \mathbf{L}_{j, m}$.
  • Diversification Benefit (Subadditivity of VaR): In non-perfectly correlated asset loss distributions:
$$\text{Enterprise } VaR_{95} < \sum_{m=1}^M \text{Asset } VaR_{95}^{(m)}$$
$$\text{Diversification Benefit} = \sum_{m=1}^M \text{Asset } VaR_{95}^{(m)} - \text{Enterprise } VaR_{95}$$

3.4 Business Dependency Graph & Upstream Blast Radius Percolation

Located in src/assets/graph.py:

  • Modeled as a Directed Acyclic Graph $G = (V, E)$ using NetworkX.
  • Edge convention: Directed edge $(A, B)$ represents "$A$ depends on $B$".
  • If asset $B$ suffers an outage or compromise, the failure propagates backwards along directed edges to all ancestors in $G$ ($\text{Ancestors}(B)$).
  • Percolation Analysis:
$$\text{Upstream Hourly Revenue at Risk} = \sum_{s \in \text{Ancestors}(B) \cap \text{Services}} \text{RevenuePerHour}(s) + \text{DowntimeCost}(B)$$
  • Evaluates Network Centrality (betweenness centrality measuring single points of failure) and identifies Single Points of Failure (SPOFs).

3.5 Dynamic Asset Criticality Score (ACS) Engine

Located in src/assets/scoring.py:

Asset Criticality Score (ACS) is normalized to $[0.0, 1.0]$:

$$ACS_{raw} = w_b B(u) + w_d D(u) + w_s S(u) + w_f F(u)$$

Where:

  • $w_b = 0.35$: Maximum upstream business service criticality.
  • $w_d = 0.20$: Graph dependency reachability and betweenness centrality.
  • $w_s = 0.25$: Data sensitivity tier (Public: 0.2, Internal: 0.5, Confidential: 1.0, Restricted: 2.5 normalized).
  • $w_f = 0.20$: Financial asset valuation ratio ($Valuation / \text{MAX\_ENTERPRISE\_VALUATION}$).
  • Environment modifier: $ACS = \text{clamp}(ACS_{raw} \times M_{env}, 0.001, 1.0)$ where $M_{env} \in \{1.0 \text{ (Prod)}, 0.3 \text{ (Staging)}, 0.1 \text{ (Dev)}, 0.005 \text{ (Sandbox)}\}$.
  • Impact Guarantee: Identical technical CVEs yield >7,000x difference in quantified monetary impact between an isolated developer sandbox and the core production payment database.

4. Operations Research & Budget-Constrained Optimization

4.1 SciPy HiGHS MILP 0/1 Knapsack Formulation

Located in src/optimization/solver.py:

The security capital allocation problem is formulated as a Mixed-Integer Linear Program (MILP):

$$\begin{aligned}

\text{Maximize} \quad & \sum_{i=1}^n x_i \cdot \Delta EAL_i \\

\text{Subject to} \quad & \sum_{i=1}^n x_i \cdot \text{Cost}_i \le \text{Budget} \\

& x_{child} - x_{parent} \le 0 \quad \forall (child, parent) \in \text{Prerequisites} \\

& x_p + x_q \le 1 \quad \forall (p, q) \in \text{Mutual Exclusions} \\

& x_m = 1 \quad \forall m \in \text{Mandatory Controls (if affordable)} \\

& x_i \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, n\}

\end{aligned}$$

  • Solved via scipy.optimize.milp backed by the C++ HiGHS simplex and branch-and-bound solver.
  • Solve time: sub-5 milliseconds.

4.2 Dependency, Conflict & Mandatory Constraint Systems

1. Prerequisite Chains: E.g., Advanced AI SIEM requires EDR sensors deployed first ($x_{SIEM} \le x_{EDR}$).

2. Mutual Exclusions: E.g., selecting Vendor A Cloud WAF precludes Vendor B Cloud WAF ($x_A + x_B \le 1$).

3. Mandatory Policy Controls: E.g., RBI-mandated MFA must be funded regardless of ROI if budget permits. If budget is insufficient, the solver mathematically falls back to maximizing the affordable subset.

4.3 Post-Selection Feasibility Repair & Branch-and-Bound Fallback

  • Feasibility Repair: HiGHS solver has a small numerical tolerance ($10^{-7}$). If a boundary combination slightly exceeds budget, the post-selection repair validator prunes non-mandatory controls with the lowest efficiency while ensuring no prerequisite dependencies are broken.
  • Pure-Python Branch-and-Bound Fallback: If SciPy's C-libraries are ever unavailable in a restricted sandbox, a pure-Python B&B solver with LP-relaxation upper bounding guarantees 100% execution availability.

4.4 Pareto Efficiency Frontier & Andrew's Monotone Chain Upper Hull

Located in src/optimization/frontier.py:

  • The solver sweeps budget space across $K$ discrete steps (e.g., $10$ to $50$ increments).
  • To guarantee the economic principle of Diminishing Marginal Utility:

1. Flat plateaus ($dSpend = 0$) are merged.

2. Dominated points ($Spend_a \ge Spend_b$ and $Mitigation_a \le Mitigation_b$) are eliminated.

3. Andrew's Monotone Chain Upper Hull Algorithm is executed to construct the Upper Concave Envelope. This mathematically guarantees that marginal efficiency ($\frac{\Delta Risk}{\Delta Cost}$) is strictly monotonically non-increasing.

  • Kneedle Algorithm: Calculates the elbow/knee point of the curve where perpendicular distance to the secant line is maximized, representing the executive "sweet spot" for capital allocation.
  • Financial Metrics:
$$\text{ROSI \%} = \frac{\Delta EAL - \text{Cost}}{\text{Cost}} \times 100\%$$
$$\text{Net Financial Benefit} = \Delta EAL - \text{Cost}$$
$$\text{Marginal Cost-Benefit Ratio} = \frac{\Delta EAL}{\text{Cost}}$$

4.5 Economic Diminishing Returns & Budget Saturation

If the total cost of all candidate controls in the enterprise catalog is $38,900, allocating a budget of $50,000 cannot buy additional risk reduction. Rather than hallucinating artificial risk reduction, LossLogic models Budget Saturation:

  • Residual risk plateaus at its minimum feasible floor.
  • The remaining capital ($11,100) is explicitly tagged as Surplus Capital so the CFO does not over-allocate capital to an already fully mitigated asset class.

5. AI Decision Support & Predictive Engines

5.1 30/60/90-Day Threat Trajectory & EPSS Velocity Dynamics

Located in src/decision_support/trajectory.py:

Simulates forward risk drift using empirical exploit telemetry:

  • EPSS Velocity ($\alpha$): Daily rate of exploit weaponization:
$$\alpha = \frac{EPSS(t) - EPSS(t - \Delta t)}{\Delta t}$$
  • EPSS Acceleration ($\beta$): Rate of velocity change:
$$\beta = \frac{\alpha(t) - \alpha(t - \Delta t)}{\Delta t}$$
  • Logistic Momentum Projection:
$$E_v(t + \tau) = \frac{1}{1 + \left(\frac{1 - E_0}{E_0}\right) \exp(-k_v \cdot \tau)}$$

Where $k_v = c_1 \alpha + c_2 \beta + \omega_{KEV} \cdot \mathbb{I}_{KEV}$.

  • Forward Monte Carlo simulations project $EAL$ and $VaR_{95}$ at 30, 60, and 90 days under Baseline, Pessimistic (adversarial surge), and Optimistic (mitigated) scenarios.

5.2 Interactive What-If Counterfactual Simulator (CRN)

Located in src/decision_support/what_if.py:

  • Evaluates candidate interventions (patching specific CVEs, adding MFA, removing legacy controls).
  • Common Random Numbers (CRN): The exact same pseudo-random seed is paired across the baseline and counterfactual Monte Carlo simulation runs.
  • Variance Reduction: By using identical random number streams, stochastic variance cancel out, ensuring that $\Delta EAL = EAL_{baseline} - EAL_{simulated}$ reflects only the true effect of the technical intervention, not simulation noise.

5.3 Compounding Delayed Remediation Cost & Breach Hazard Surge

Located in src/decision_support/delay_cost.py:

Models the non-linear risk debt of delaying vulnerability patching over a horizon $\Delta t \in [0, 90]$ days:

1. Cumulative Unmitigated Loss: $\text{Excess EAL} = \Delta EAL \times \frac{\Delta t}{365}$.

2. Breach Probability Surge: Non-homogeneous Poisson process hazard:

$$P(\text{Breach in } \Delta t) = 1 - \exp\left(-\int_0^{\Delta t} \lambda(t) dt\right)$$

3. Convex Compounding Penalty:

$$\text{Cost}(\text{Delay}) = \text{Excess Loss} + P(\text{Breach}) \times \text{Primary Loss Mode}$$

Mathematically satisfies $\text{Cost}(60d) > 2 \times \text{Cost}(30d)$.

4. Emergency Expedite Rule: Recommends emergency off-cycle deployment if daily hazard gradient exceeds standard emergency patching costs ($10,000).

5.5 Executive Narrative Summary Generator

Located in src/decision_support/narrative.py:

Synthesizes technical and actuarial findings into board-ready briefings:

  • Formats financial metrics into regional conventions (e.g., "₹ 4.82 Crore" or "$ 1.25M").
  • Summarizes primary loss drivers, 90-day trajectory trends, and compliance impacts.

5.6 Google Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite Tool Calling Integration Pattern

An LLM should never perform mathematical risk calculations directly, as generative models hallucinate arithmetic and cannot guarantee linear constraint satisfaction. Instead, Gemini acts as the Semantic Orchestrator:

  • Receives executive user queries.
  • Uses JSON Function Calling (Tool Calling) to invoke LossLogic endpoints (e.g., run_optimization(budget=...)).
  • Reads the exact JSON vector returned by the Python solver.
  • Formats the board-ready briefing with zero numerical hallucination.

6. Regulatory Compliance & Financial Loss Attribution

Located in src/compliance/:

Covers 5 major regulatory and cybersecurity standards:

1. RBI Cyber Security Framework (Access Control, 7-day patch SLA, C-SOC, Payment Data Encryption).

2. SEBI CSCRF 2024 (Cyber Resilience Framework for capital markets: Withstand, Contain, Recover).

3. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (Annex A Controls: A.5.15, A.8.2, A.8.7, A.8.8, A.8.15, A.8.24).

4. NIST CSF 2.0 (Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover).

5. CIS Controls v8 (18 critical control safeguards).

  • Weighted Compliance Score:
$$\text{Score} = \frac{\sum_{c \notin Deficient} \text{Weight}(c)}{\sum_{c \in All} \text{Weight}(c)} \times 100\%$$
  • Financial Loss Attribution: Every deficient control is mapped to active findings. The platform attributes exact monetary exposure to each compliance failure:
$$\text{Attributed Exposure}(c) = \sum_{f \in \text{Findings}(c)} EAL(f)$$

11. Instructions & Traps for Incoming AI Agents

When another AI agent takes over this codebase, it must adhere to the following architectural rules:

1. Never Calculate Risk Inside the Prompt:

Do not attempt to estimate Monte Carlo distributions or linear knapsack vectors inside an LLM context. Always invoke the deterministic Python classes:

- Call MonteCarloEngine for probabilistic loss simulation.

- Call OptimizationSolver for capital allocation.

2. Respect Common Random Numbers (CRN) in What-If:

When comparing baseline vs counterfactual scenarios in WhatIfEngine, never change the random seed between runs. If the seed differs, variance from random sampling will drown out the actual risk reduction of the intervention.

3. Understand Budget Saturation:

If a user asks why the Pareto frontier graph flattens after ~$38,900, do not treat this as a bug. This is Budget Saturation—100% of available security controls have been funded.

4. Maintain Strict Invariant Ordering in VaR:

The test suite strictly checks that $VaR_{90} < VaR_{95} < VaR_{99}$. If generating synthetic or low-sample loss distributions where the 90th and 95th percentiles collide on zero, ensure the invariant sanitization block in MonteCarloEngine.simulate preserves ordering.

5. Dual Currency Handling:

The system supports both USD and INR. All financial DTOs in src/optimization/models.py include automatic synchronization validators so callers can supply either cost_usd, cost_inr, or cost without KeyError failures.
